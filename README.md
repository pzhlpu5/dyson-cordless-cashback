## Which one should you actually buy?

Reviews are consistent enough to draw some lines here.

**Best overall performer:** the Gen5detect. In Wired's side-by-side testing across five rounds, it came out with a near-perfect score, handling carpets, hard floors and sand better than anything else in the lineup, and pulling the most cat hair out of carpet thanks to 280 air watts [1]. It also has the washable HEPA filter, which the V15 doesn't, and a power button rather than a trigger you have to hold.

**Best value for most homes:** the V15 Detect. RTINGS rates it the best Dyson it has tested because it performs well on every surface, and notes the Gen5detect doesn't improve real-world cleaning enough to justify roughly US$200 more for a typical home [7]. In Wired's tests it finished right behind the Gen5detect, with slightly more dust left behind on hard floors [1].

**Best budget pick:** the V8 Cyclone at US$400, or the V10 Konical at US$500. Wired found the V10 Konical never once finished last in its tests and was more comfortable to push around on rugs than the more expensive V15 and V8 [1]. RTINGS' position on the V8 is that it's the best budget Dyson stick, lighter than the V15, and the difference between 115 AW and 230 AW rarely matters in a small flat or as a second vacuum [7].

**Best for small flats and stairs:** the V12 Detect Slim. At around 2.2 kg it's the lightest Dyson with a laser, which is why it keeps coming up for apartment dwellers. The trade-off is a 0.35 L bin, so you'll empty it more often.

One practical annoyance that shows up in owner forums more than in reviews: the V15 uses a trigger you hold down. If you're vacuuming a whole house, that gets tiring, and the Gen5detect, V12 and V8 Cyclone all moved to a button.

## What a Dyson cordless vacuum costs — and why the sticker varies so much

Dyson does not run one global price. Two things drive the spread.

First, currency and VAT. The V15 Detect Absolute carries a €799 list price in the European Dyson store [3], and SGD 1,349 for the HEPA version in Singapore against SGD 1,299 for the standard filtration model [2]. Same machine platform, a few hundred dollars apart depending on where you live and which filter you pick.

Second, Dyson's discount behaviour differs by market. In Singapore, Dyson holds retail firm across authorised channels — the official store, department stores, airport duty-free and marketplace official stores all list at the same SGD price. What changes is the incentive on top: cashback or a gift-with-purchase bundle [2][5]. In Australia, Dyson's end-of-financial-year sale has cut up to A$555 off vacuums, and the December 2025 run dropped the V15 Detect Absolute to A$676 with a promo code — well under its A$1,449 list [6][8].

That last example is worth pausing on, because it's also how people accidentally lose their cashback. More on that below.

Worth knowing: the V15 Detect Absolute is sold in HEPA and non-HEPA versions in Singapore, and the HEPA model filters 99.99% of particles down to 0.1 microns for about SGD 50 more [2]. If there's an allergy or a shedding pet in the house, that's cheap insurance. If not, the standard version is the same motor and the same laser.

## Warranty, and why the cheapest listing usually isn't

Dyson's authorised channels carry a two-year warranty covering the motor, battery pack and manufacturing defects — in Singapore that's the official store, department stores, airport duty-free and the brand's marketplace official stores [2][5]. Grey-market listings on secondhand platforms and unauthorised marketplace sellers often don't, and Dyson won't honour warranty claims on units sourced outside its authorised network.

A five to fifteen percent discount from a grey-market seller is roughly what you'd get back in cashback from an authorised purchase anyway — except the grey-market route also costs you the warranty on a machine with a battery and a motor. Battery failure is the most commonly reported long-term Dyson problem, which makes that trade an easy call for most people.
